# The Rahal Show: Monetization, Partnerships & Sponsorship Strategy

*Prepared 20 October 2026. Built from the pitch deck and four research reports in [`research/`](research/): creators, market, monetization, partnerships. Figures marked **[EST]** are our planning estimates, not reported data. Every rate in this document must be checked against the Rahals' own deal history and 2–3 agency quotes before it goes into a sponsor deck.*

---

## 1. The thesis

**Sell The Rahal Show as a branded-entertainment platform with TV-grade production. Do not sell it as an influencer channel that happens to have a long-form show.**

Three facts drive everything below:

1. **YouTube ads will not fund this show.** Arabic ad rates are low: about $3.9 per 1,000 views in the UAE, $2.0 in KSA and $0.7 in Egypt. A 300K-view episode earns roughly **$300–900** from ads **[EST]**. Hot Ones gets only ~15% of its revenue from ads.
2. **The format is already a stack of sellable units.** It has 8 segments: a trailer, 3 sketches, a conversation, 2 games and an outro. On top of that come a set that can carry products, and 6–10 short clips per episode. Each one can be sold, the same way Fallon sold games to GE, Ford and Amazon.
3. **Celebrity guests are both the biggest asset and the biggest risk.**
   - **Risk:** every guest arrives with their own exclusive brand deals. Nancy Ajram is tied to Tiffany and has a long Coca-Cola history. Amr Diab and Mo Salah are Pepsi faces, and Diab is also an OPPO ambassador. Karen Wazen owns an eyewear brand.
   - **Opportunity:** every guest also arrives with a project to promote and brands that might pay to be in the room.
   - **Conclusion:** the strategy has to defuse the first and charge for the second.

**Target:** pre-sell **at least 80% of each season's production budget** before filming starts. Sponsorship and branded content will make up **70–85% of Year-1 revenue** **[EST]**. Everything else adds to that or builds long-term IP value.

- **YouTube is their smallest platform** (~700K subscribers), and there is no long-form track record.
- **Audience location is unknown.** We need the split between KSA, the UAE and the Levant.
- **They have no confirmed celebrity network yet.** Nancy, Haifa and Karen are names on a wish list, not bookings.

## 3. How the show earns: five layers of brand money

```
LAYER 1  SEASON TITLE PARTNER         1 brand, one category to itself, all season
LAYER 2  SEGMENT PARTNERS             3–5 brands, each owns a game or segment
LAYER 3  SET & PRODUCTION PARTNERS    furniture, appliances, wardrobe, beverages (barter + fee)
LAYER 4  EPISODE PARTNERS             guest's own brand, guest's project, sponsored sketch
LAYER 5  SEASONAL TENTPOLES           Ramadan Nights · Riyadh Season · White Friday · DSF
```

### 3.2 Rate card anchors [EST, check before pitching]

Starting point: a single Rahal Reel or TikTok is estimated at **AED 30K–80K**. CreatorDB estimates $17K–40K per Instagram post.

| Package | Indicative price [EST] | What's included |
|---|---|---|
| **Season Title Partner** (10 episodes) | **AED 1.0M–1.8M** | Opening and closing cards, 1 host-read per episode, logo on every clip, Fake Unboxing delivery role, 2 dedicated social posts, exclusive category, brand-lift study |
| **Segment Partner** | **AED 50K–120K per episode**, sold in 5-episode blocks | Named segment, product in play, 2–3 clips per episode carrying the brand |
| **Sponsored Sketch** | **AED 120K–250K per sketch** | Custom scripted sketch (the Paramount model) plus clip cuts. Premium because it is produced. |
| **Home & Set Partner** | Set built in kind + **AED 150K–300K per season** | Permanent on-screen credit, shoppable "get the look" content |
| **Bring-Your-Brand Episode** | **AED 150K–300K per episode** | The guest's own ambassador brand co-funds the episode (section 5.2) |
| **Release Partner** | **AED 75K–200K**, or paid in production or promotion | The studio, label or platform behind the guest's new project (section 5.2) |
| **Ramadan Nights Presenting Partner** | **AED 1.5M–2.5M** | 4 long-form celebrity specials plus *Fawazir Rahal* S5 daily (section 6) |
| **Add-ons** | Whitelisting (brand runs our content as paid ads): **+20–25% per 30 days**. Exclusive category: **+15–30%**. Clips performing above target: view bonus. | Never include usage rights for free |

**Sell bundles, not single slots.** Every package includes the long episode plus clips on Shorts, TikTok, Reels and Snapchat. Snapchat reaches more than 90% of Saudis aged 13–34. Gulf brands are used to buying reach on Instagram and TikTok. The long episode is what makes that reach premium and safe for TV-style advertisers.

### 3.3 Three ideas that set the show apart

1. **"Rahal Mart", a made-up brand universe for Fake Unboxing and the sketches.** UAE trademark law has no parody defence, so mocking a real brand is a legal risk. Invented products ("Abu Rahal Anti-Gossip Spray") can never conflict with a guest's deals. They become recurring jokes, and they can later be sold as real merch. SNL's fake ads work the same way.
2. **"Roast My Brand", a premium paid segment.** A brand pays to be roasted by the guest and the hosts. Self-mocking brand marketing performs well globally, and because the brand consents in writing, the legal risk is low. Price it above a sponsored sketch.
3. **Turn the props into products.** Blender Confessions becomes a co-branded "Confession Blender" or smoothie line with the appliance partner, with a licensing royalty to the show. Hot Ones did this with its Last Dab sauce.

## 5. Celebrity guests: protecting sponsors and earning from guests

### 5.1 Protecting sponsors from guest conflicts

**The problem:** each episode places one celebrity at the centre of a room full of brands. Ambassador contracts usually include **category exclusivity**, limits on appearing next to competitors, and **morals clauses**. If a guest breaches them, the guest's agent bears the cost. In practice the agent then vetoes the booking or demands sponsor assets be removed, so the show carries the risk.

**Where the clashes are** (details in [`research/04-partnerships.md`](research/04-partnerships.md)):

| Category | Clashes with | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Soft drinks | Pepsi: Amr Diab (2026 World Cup ad), Mo Salah, Elissa (unconfirmed). Coca-Cola: Nancy Ajram (long-time face). Plus boycott sentiment against both. | 🔴 **Never a season sponsor.** Regional drinks only. |
| Jewellery | Nancy (Tiffany, Damas), Myriam Fares (L'azurde), Nadine Njeim (MARLI), Balqees (Piaget, Marli) | 🔴 **Never a season sponsor.** Episode-level only. |
| Smartphones | Amr Diab (OPPO), Mo Salah (OPPO, reported) | 🔴 **Never a season sponsor.** |
| Telcos | Ahmed Saad (Zain, Orange), Ahmed Helmy (Etisalat Misr), Mo Salah (Vodafone) | 🟡 Segment only, with an exception for any episode where it clashes |
| Beauty and fragrance | Balqees (Guerlain), Karen Wazen (fragrance history, her own eyewear brand) | 🟡 Segment only, with an exception for any episode where it clashes |
| Real estate | Amr Diab (One Development) | 🟡 |
| Music streaming | Haifa Wehbe (Spotify EQUAL ambassador) | 🟡 Watch the Anghami podcast deal on her episode |
| Delivery, appliances, home, coffee, tourism, Chinese cars, regional banks | No conflicts found | 🟢 **Season-level categories** |

**How the show handles it (the "clean-room protocol"):**

1. **Get each guest's conflicts in writing before choosing sponsors.** Every booking includes a *Category Clearance Form*: the guest lists their exclusive categories and confirms the list is accurate, so liability sits with the guest. We have found no public endorsement list that is fully up to date.
2. **Sponsors attach to segments, not to the guest.** Host-read spots are done by Miled and Melissa only, never the guest, as Fallon does. This also avoids needing a UAE Advertiser Permit or Saudi Mawthooq licence for the guest.
3. **Make two cuts of every clip.** One version carries sponsor branding for the Rahal channels. A clean version without branding goes to the guest for their own collab post. The guest's huge audience then spreads the show without breaking their contracts.
4. **Keep a "swap kit".** Every branded prop on set has an unbranded twin: the blender, mugs, drinks, the TV. When a guest clashes, the swap takes about 10 minutes.
5. **Own the props.** Rahal Mart products and show-branded mugs and cups mean the core set has no third-party products to clash with. This is the Hot Ones lesson.
6. **Write the sponsor contracts for this.** Include:
   - an **episode exception clause**, so the show can drop a sponsor's on-set presence for a clashing guest;
   - a **make-good** when that happens (bonus clips, or credit toward another episode);
   - **2 weeks' notice of each guest**, with **no sponsor approval** over guests;
   - a **"kill switch"**, so a sponsor can pull out of a single episode if that guest is caught in a scandal before it airs.
   - Keep one **reserve episode** ready to replace it.

### 5.3 Guest tiers and season casting

| Tier | Who | Role in the season | Cost and risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Headliners** | A-list: Nancy Ajram, Haifa Wehbe, Elissa, Amr Diab, Assala | 2–3 per season: **premiere, mid-season, finale** | Appearance fee likely (MENA A-listers rarely appear free). Heaviest conflict checks. |
| **2. Stars of the moment** | Leads of the current Ramadan series, comedians, Saudi stars | 4–5 per season. Natural Release Partner bookings. | Moderate. Often funded by their promoters. |
| **3. Internet royalty** | Top regional creators, athletes, viral personalities | 2–3 per season. High clip virality. | Low fee. Collab reach. |

**Season 1 casting rule:** confirm one Tier-1 headliner for the premiere before selling the title partner. Shoot the pilot or sizzle reel with a Tier-2 or Tier-3 guest to prove the format first. **Mix the guests across the Levant, Egypt and the Gulf in every season**, with at least two Saudi guests.

**Appearance fees:** no public MENA benchmarks exist. Budget a fee range for each tier and agree it with talent managers. Bring-Your-Brand and Release Partners should cover all or most of the fee.

### 5.4 Keeping the comedy legal and brand-safe

UAE law treats online insult as a criminal matter (Cybercrime Law Art. 43: prison and/or AED 250K–500K fines, and truth is not a reliable defence). The Media Council standards allow fines up to **AED 1M**. **No sponsor will stay attached to an episode that makes the news for the wrong reasons.** The rules:

- **The Roast and Mean Comments:** the guest signs off in writing on a topic list and a list of topics that are off limits. Only the consenting guest is roasted. **Never name a third party** (exes, rivals, other celebrities). Real commenters are anonymised. No comments about religion, nationality, ethnicity, family, or appearance combined with sexuality.
- **Guest Trailer:** use only "scandals" the guest has acknowledged publicly and approves. Never allege wrongdoing. Any AI-generated likeness of a public figure needs prior approval from the UAE National Media Authority.
- **No-go topics:** God, religion and prophets; rulers, royal families, states and national symbols; politics (Hicham Haddad was prosecuted in 2018 over a joke about Mohammed bin Salman); mocking a whole nationality or dialect.
- **Categories the show never accepts:** alcohol, gambling and betting, crypto, unlicensed health or weight-loss products, and brands exposed to the boycott at title level.
- **Ramadan edit:** softer tone, less suggestive physical comedy, sponsor categories suited to families.
- **Process:** a written brand-safety checklist based on the 20 UAE content standards, plus a legal read of every episode before publication. **Present this to sponsors as a feature.** Banks, telcos and government partners need that assurance.

**Licences:**
- UAE **Advertiser Permit** for the production company and both hosts (mandatory since about February 2026).
- Saudi **Mawthooq** (~SAR 15K for 3 years). Non-Saudis need a licensed agency. It is required for any sponsor content aimed at KSA, and visiting celebrities doing paid promotion are a stated enforcement focus.

## 8. Getting the most out of each episode

- **Home:** the long-form show lives on **@therahal**, which is already monetized, with a "The Rahal Show" playlist and podcast. A new channel would start from zero.
- **Release time:** Thursday 9pm KSA as a Premiere. Release after iftar during Ramadan.
- **One episode produces:**
  - 1 long-form episode;
  - 6–10 vertical clips, each sketch and game standalone, across Shorts, TikTok, Reels and Snapchat;
  - 1 clean collab clip for the guest;
  - The Conversation as audio and video podcast (Anghami, Spotify, Apple);
  - English and French AI-dubbed audio tracks, with human-checked subtitles for punchlines;
  - Rahal Mart and sponsor clips for paid amplification.
- **Sponsor reporting:** a live report for each partner showing engaged views (the honest Shorts metric since March 2025), long-form watch time, audience by country, YouTube Brand Lift, and promo-code and tracking-link sales.

## 9. Business set-up

1. **Separate company for the IP.** The show, format bible, segment names and Rahal Mart belong to a dedicated company, separate from the couple's personal accounts. All trademarks registered in the UAE and KSA. **Never give a sponsor or platform ownership of the format.** Format IP is what made First We Feast worth $82.5M.
2. **Decide who sells.** The Rahals run their own agency (therahal.com). Agree who sells the inventory: in-house, an appointed agency, or a regional sales house such as SRMG Media Solutions or ITP Live, possibly with a minimum guarantee. Agree how revenue and commissions are split.
3. **Reduce dependence on the couple.** Build a writers' room, recurring supporting characters and formats that are owned assets: games, Rahal Mart, the Trailer franchise. Use clear contracts between the two hosts, the company and the IP. Khalid Al Ameri and Salama are the regional cautionary example.
4. **Respect the Rahals' values.** They publicly refuse products they wouldn't rate highly and keep their children off camera. Keep the sponsor list curated (one brand per category per season), and never sell an integration that needs the children.
