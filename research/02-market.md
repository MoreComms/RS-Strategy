### 1.2 YouTube penetration, watch time and CTV
- **CTV reach (May 2024, adults 18+):** YouTube on connected TV reached **>12M people in KSA, >2.5M in UAE, 600K in Qatar** (YouTube Brandcast, Oct 2024). Source: [Google MENA blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-mena/product-updates/connect-communicate/viewers-in-saudi-arabia-and-uae-choose-youtube-as-their-1-platform-advertisers/); [Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/uae-saudi-qatar-viewers-shift-to-watching-youtube-on-connected-tv/). *I found no newer country CTV count in public sources.*
- **#1 platform:** A Kantar survey in KSA/UAE/Qatar found viewers ranked YouTube as their top video platform, and Kantar ranks it the #1 platform for brand research in KSA and UAE (Brandcast 2024/2025).
- **Shorts vs Reels (Brandcast Nov 2025, GWI):** **61% of Saudi** and **54% of UAE** YouTube Shorts users do not use Instagram Reels, so Shorts reaches an audience that IG does not. YouTube also launched Veo 3 in Shorts and Arabic auto-dubbing (already used by 51K Egyptian creators, Egyptian Arabic to English) and announced a **Creator Partnerships Hub for brands in 2026**. Source: [Al Bawaba](https://www.albawaba.com/business/pr/youtube-launch-veo-3-shorts-mena-new-1616282), [Cairoscene](https://cairoscene.com/Buzz/YouTube-To-Roll-Out-Veo-3-On-Shorts-In-Select-MENA-Countries).
- **Co-viewing:** In a 2025 Kantar Ramadan survey, **60% of UAE viewers** said they watch YouTube with friends or family. Source: [Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/capitalising-on-ramadans-digital-shift-towards-connected-tv/).
- **Watch time:** The last public daily figures are old (Kantar, 2020 data: ~70 min/day UAE, ~55 min/day KSA) and should be treated as **outdated**.
- **Creator earnings growth (YouTube, Apr 2025, as of Dec 2024):** channels earning 7 figures in local currency rose **+40% in KSA (SAR)**, **+60% in Egypt (EGP)** and **+15% in UAE (AED)**. **More than 95% of watch time on UAE-based channels comes from outside the UAE.** For Egyptian channels, more than 60% of views come from abroad. Sources: [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-reports-record-growth-in-mena-creator-earnings-on-20th-anniversary/), [Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/youtube-turns-20-and-reveals-insights-on-creator-economy-growth-in-mena/). **Implication:** a Dubai channel is in practice a pan-Arab export channel, and its AdSense yield follows where viewers are (KSA/Egypt/diaspora), not the UAE.

### 2.3 Licensing and compliance (critical for paid integrations)
- **UAE Advertiser Permit (Media Council):** mandatory from **~Feb 1, 2026** for anyone in the UAE who advertises on social media, **paid or unpaid** (citizens, residents and visitors). It is free for the first 3 years, and the permit number must appear on the account. Exemptions: promoting your own business on your personal account, and under-18s doing educational, cultural or sports activity ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/new-uae-law-advertiser-permit-now-mandatory-for-influencers-and-creators-for-social-media-1.500427938), [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2025/07/30/uae-social-media-advertiser-permit/)). Reported fines range from AED 10K for a missing permit up to AED 1M under the media law. One secondary source cites AED 2M for repeat offenders, which is unverified.
- **UAE Media Law content standards (Cabinet Res. 42/2025, effective May 29, 2025):** 20 standards apply to creators. Disrespecting God, Islam or other religions falls in the top tier (up to **AED 1M**). Disrespecting national symbols, state institutions or governance carries AED 50K–500K. Repeat violations double the fine ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/uae-media-law-media-must-follow-20-key-standards-to-avoid-fines-of-up-to-dh1m-1.500157204), [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/life-and-living/influencer-fines-content-creator-full-list-2025?amp=1)).
- **Saudi Mawthooq licence (GAMR, formerly GCAM):** required to publish paid content aimed at KSA. The fee is **SAR 15,000 for 3 years**. **Non-Saudis** generally must go through the Ministry of Investment and a licensed agency. Brands are warned not to use unlicensed creators, **including visiting celebrities**. Penalties reach up to SAR 1M plus bans (some sources cite higher) ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2176661/media), [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-tightens-rules-on-influencer-ads-with-mandatory-licensing-1.1733131320843), [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2022/09/new-social-media-requirements-for-saudi-arabia)). *I could not find a confirmed 2025 fee update; verify with GAMR.*

### 4.3 Cultural, religious and brand-safety sensitivities
- **Hard no's (legal risk in UAE/KSA):** mocking God, religion or prophets; insulting royals or rulers, governments, national symbols or state institutions; promoting **alcohol, gambling or betting**; positive LGBTQ portrayal; explicit sexual content. The Riyadh Comedy Festival contract barred material that "degrades… Saudi Arabia, its royal family… or any religion" ([Chortle](https://www.chortle.co.uk/news/2025/09/28/59143/revealed%3A_the_rules_riyadh_festival_comics_must_follow)). Bill Burr summarised the rule as "don't make fun of royals [and] religion".
- **Grey zones:** dating and romance jokes (fine within marriage, and the Rahals' married-couple framing is an advantage here); mixed-gender physical comedy; mocking regional dialects or nationalities, especially Saudi, Emirati or Egyptian (punch up, never at a nation); celebrity "scandals" in the Guest Trailer, which needs guest sign-off and must avoid defamation; Lebanese political humour (see Haddad).
- **Ramadan editing:** use a softer tone, avoid music-heavy or suggestive bits, and expect more conservative sponsors (telcos, banks, F&B, government).

## 5. Competitive white space

1. **No Arabic "Hot Ones / Fallon" hybrid.** Arabic celebrity long-form is split between **serious interviews** (ABtalks, Fnjan, Seen), **once-a-year Ramadan TV pranks** (Ramez Galal), and **faceless sketch networks** (Telfaz11, UTURN). We found no evidence of a **weekly, YouTube-first, scripted-sketch + games + interview show** starring a celebrity guest.
2. **Couple-hosted comedy.** No major Arabic celebrity show uses a **married duo** as hosts. A married couple makes relationship comedy culturally safe and widens appeal to family co-viewing (60% of UAE YouTube viewing is co-viewed).
3. **Pan-Arab rather than single-market.** Saudi shows skew Khaleeji and Egyptian shows skew Egyptian. Lebanese hosts based in Dubai and working in white or Levantine Arabic, with Gulf, Egyptian and Levantine guests, can travel across markets. That matters because more than 95% of UAE-channel watch time is international.
4. **Built-in integration slots.** "Fake Unboxing AD", "Blender Confessions" and a 70s set that can carry product placement are native sponsor inventory that most Arabic talk shows lack. They fit the regional shift from paying for reach to paying for performance and integration.
5. **Celebrity "soft side" content for brands.** Brands buy Ramez-style celebrity humiliation every Ramadan at TV prices. A kinder, brand-safe, year-round version on YouTube, CTV and social clips is an unfilled slot.
6. **Gaps confirmed by the market:** Chicken Shop Date is ending (Sep 2026), YouTube is launching a Creator Partnerships Hub (2026), and IAB reports CTV growing 31%. Taken together, these suggest a window is opening for premium creator-led celebrity formats watched on the living-room TV.

## Key implications for The Rahal Show monetization

1. **Build the business on brand partnerships, not AdSense.** Arabic RPMs are low and vary widely by geo (estimates range from about $0.3 for Egypt to about $2 or more for UAE/KSA). Plan for AdSense to be under about 15–20% of revenue, matching Hot Ones' ~15% ad share (analyst estimate). Sell **season title sponsorship + segment sponsorships** (e.g. "Blender Confessions presented by [appliance brand]", "Fake Unboxing" rotating brands, set and furniture placement). Fallon/GE and Fallon/Amazon are the proof of concept.
2. **Price for KSA.** KSA is the largest ad market (≈$4.2B) and the largest influencer market, with the largest CTV audience (12M+). Book Saudi guests, use Khaleeji-friendly sketches, and court Saudi sponsors (stc, SNB, STA, F&B, telcos). **Obtain a Mawthooq licence (or work through a licensed Saudi agency) before selling KSA-targeted integrations**, and require that guests are licensed too.
3. **Compliance as a selling point.** Hold **UAE Advertiser Permits** (mandatory since about Feb 2026), display permit numbers on accounts, and run content through a written brand-safety checklist based on the 20 UAE standards. Tell brands about it. Blue-chip advertisers (banks, telcos, government) need this assurance.
4. **Treat Ramadan as the tentpole.** Run a **Ramadan special season or daily mini-series** (post-iftar release, 8pm–2am peak) sold as a premium package. Ramadan holds about 20–30% of annual ad spend and is projected above $2B in 2026. Add Eid and White Friday packages and a **Riyadh Season partnership** (GEA- or sponsor-funded episodes, as with Podcast Big Time).
5. **Design for TV screens.** CTV is the fastest-growing format (+31%), so the 4K long-form episode should be packaged for CTV ad buys and **licensing or windowing to Shahid or OSN+**. Shahid is the largest SVOD (about 4.4M subscribers). A YouTube-first + Shahid second-window deal is a realistic additional revenue stream.
6. **Use clips to reach audiences IG does not.** 61% of Saudi Shorts users are not on Reels, and Snapchat reaches more than 90% of young Saudis. Sell **cross-platform bundles** (episode + 6–10 clips on Shorts, TikTok, Reels and Snap) priced above the vendor-estimated single-post rate of AED 18–44K for macro creators.
7. **Own a franchise asset.** Hot Ones' sauce shows that one recurring segment can become a product or IP (a branded game, merchandise, a live tour at Riyadh Season or Dubai venues, or a licensed format). Later, consider membership (YouTube channel memberships or a Dropout-style tier) for bonus sketches.
8. **Use Dubai incentives.** Apply to **Creators HQ** (AED 150M fund, Golden Visa support), pitch at the **1 Billion Followers Summit (Jan)**, and target UAE government and tourism partners (DET, DSF).
9. **Have guardrails before launch.** No alcohol or gambling sponsors. No jokes about royals, religion or politics. The Guest Trailer roast needs the guest's written approval. Use a softer edit for Ramadan. A single violation can mean AED 1M or SAR 1M fines and lost sponsors.
