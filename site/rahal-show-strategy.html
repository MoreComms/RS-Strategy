01The thesis

Sell The Rahal Show as a branded-entertainment platform with TV-grade production. Do not sell it as an influencer channel that happens to have a long-form show.

YouTube ads won't fund it

Arabic ads pay about $3.9 per 1,000 views in the UAE, $2.0 in KSA and $0.7 in Egypt. A 300K-view episode earns about $300–900 from ads EST. Hot Ones gets only about 15% of its revenue from ads.

The format is a stack of sellable units

8 segments, a set that can carry products, and 6–10 clips per episode. Each can be sold, as Fallon sold games to GE, Ford and Amazon.

Celebrity guests cut both ways

Every guest arrives with exclusive brand deals and a project to promote. The strategy defuses the deals and charges for the promotion.

Target: pre-sell at least 80% of each season's production budget before filming starts. Sponsorship and branded content will be about 70–85% of Year-1 revenue.

Sell bundles, not single slots. Every package includes the long episode plus clips on Shorts, TikTok, Reels and Snapchat. Snapchat reaches more than 90% of Saudis aged 13–34. The long episode is what makes that reach premium and safe for TV-style advertisers.

Three ideas that set the show apart

Rahal Mart

A made-up brand universe for Fake Unboxing and the sketches. UAE trademark law has no parody defence, so invented products avoid legal risk and can't clash with a guest's deals. They become recurring jokes and, later, real merch.

Roast My Brand

A premium paid segment: a brand pays to be roasted by the guest and hosts. The brand consents in writing, so legal risk is low. Self-mocking brand marketing performs well. Priced above a sponsored sketch.

Props into products

Blender Confessions becomes a co-branded "Confession Blender" or smoothie line with the appliance partner, with a licensing royalty to the show. Hot Ones did this with its Last Dab sauce.

05Celebrity guests: protecting sponsors and earning from guests

Each episode places one celebrity at the centre of a room full of brands. Ambassador contracts usually include category exclusivity, limits on appearing next to competitors, and morals clauses. If a guest breaches them, the guest's agent bears the cost. In practice the agent vetoes the booking or demands sponsor assets be removed, so the show carries the risk.

How the show handles it: the clean-room protocol

  1. Get each guest's conflicts in writing before choosing sponsors. Every booking includes a Category Clearance Form: the guest lists their exclusive categories and confirms the list is accurate, so liability sits with the guest. No public endorsement list is fully up to date.
  2. Sponsors attach to segments, not to the guest. Host-read spots are done by Miled and Melissa only, as Fallon does. This also avoids needing a UAE Advertiser Permit or Saudi Mawthooq licence for the guest.
  3. Make two cuts of every clip. One version carries sponsor branding for the Rahal channels. A clean version without branding goes to the guest for their collab post. The guest's audience spreads the show without breaking their contracts.
  4. Keep a swap kit. Every branded prop has an unbranded twin (blender, mugs, drinks, TV), so a clash takes about 10 minutes to fix.
  5. Own the props. Rahal Mart products and show-branded cups mean the core set has no third-party products to clash with. This is the Hot Ones lesson.
  6. Write the sponsor contracts for this. Include an episode exception clause, a make-good when it's used, 2 weeks' notice of each guest with no sponsor approval over guests, and a kill switch so a sponsor can pull out of one episode if the guest is caught in a scandal before it airs. Keep one reserve episode ready.

Keeping the comedy legal and brand-safe

UAE law treats online insult as a criminal matter (Cybercrime Law Art. 43: prison and/or AED 250K–500K fines, and truth is not a reliable defence). Media Council standards allow fines up to AED 1M. No sponsor will stay attached to an episode that makes the news for the wrong reasons.

  • The Roast and Mean Comments: the guest signs off in writing on a topic list and a list of off-limits topics. Only the consenting guest is roasted. Never name a third party. Real commenters are anonymised. No comments about religion, nationality, ethnicity, family, or appearance combined with sexuality.
  • Guest Trailer: only "scandals" the guest has acknowledged publicly and approves. Never allege wrongdoing. An AI-generated likeness of a public figure needs prior approval from the National Media Authority.
  • No-go topics: religion; rulers, royals and states; politics (Hicham Haddad was prosecuted in 2018 over a joke about Mohammed bin Salman); mocking whole nationalities or dialects.
  • Categories the show never accepts: alcohol, gambling, crypto, unlicensed health or weight-loss products, and boycott-exposed brands at title level.
  • Ramadan edit: softer tone and sponsor categories suited to families.
  • Licences: UAE Advertiser Permit for the production company and both hosts (mandatory since about February 2026). Saudi Mawthooq (~SAR 15K for 3 years, through a licensed agency for non-Saudis) for any sponsor content aimed at KSA.
  • Process: a written brand-safety checklist based on the 20 UAE content standards and a legal read of every episode. Present it to sponsors as a feature.

08Getting the most out of each episode

  • Home: long-form lives on the already-monetized @therahal channel with a "The Rahal Show" playlist and podcast. A new channel would start from zero.
  • Release time: Thursday 9pm KSA as a Premiere; after iftar during Ramadan.
  • One episode produces: the long-form episode; 6–10 vertical clips across Shorts, TikTok, Reels and Snapchat; a clean collab clip for the guest; The Conversation as a podcast (Anghami, Spotify, Apple); English and French AI-dubbed tracks with human-checked subtitles for punchlines; Rahal Mart and sponsor clips for paid amplification.
  • Sponsor reporting: a live report for each partner with engaged views, long-form watch time, audience by country, YouTube Brand Lift, and promo-code and tracking-link sales.

09Business set-up

  • A separate company owns the IP. The show, format bible, segment names and Rahal Mart sit in a dedicated company, with trademarks registered in the UAE and KSA. Never give a sponsor or platform ownership of the format. Format IP is what made First We Feast worth $82.5M.
  • Decide who sells. The Rahals run their own agency. Agree whether inventory is sold in-house, by an appointed agency, or by a regional sales house (SRMG Media Solutions, ITP Live), possibly with a minimum guarantee.
  • Reduce dependence on the couple. A writers' room, recurring characters, owned games and the Trailer franchise, plus clear contracts between the hosts, the company and the IP.
  • Respect the Rahals' values. Their public "trust first" product filter and their rule of keeping the children off camera mean one curated brand per category per season, and no integration that needs the children.
